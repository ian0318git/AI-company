### High-Demand Specializations in Embedded Medical Devices

1. **Ultra-low-power embedded systems engineers** -- Bluetooth LE, biosignal processing, wireless body-area networks
2. **Firmware engineers for medical wearables** -- ingestible capsules, continuous glucose monitors, ECG patches
3. **Biosignal processing algorithm engineers** -- real-time sensor data interpretation
4. **Regulatory affairs specialists** with dual EU MDR + FDA 510(k) experience -- the hardest roles to fill
5. **AI/ML engineers** for medical device diagnostics -- growing rapidly but talent pool is shallow

### Talent Market Dynamics

- **Time-to-fill for medtech roles**: average **94 days** (vs. 46 days for general software)
- **Senior-level candidate availability**: >90% of candidates are passive; job postings reach only ~10% of viable talent
- **Embedded systems engineers**: effectively **zero unemployment** in regions like the Nordics
- **Notified body audit backlogs**: 8--14 months for Class IIa/IIb devices, creating compliance hiring pressure

### Key Employment Trends for Atmo Biosciences Context

- **Ingestible sensor companies** like Atmo directly compete for the same ultra-low-power embedded systems talent pool as the broader medtech wearable sector
- **Regulatory expertise** is the #1 bottleneck -- companies with FDA 510(k)-cleared products (like Atmo) have a talent advantage in attracting regulatory specialists
- **AI integration in capsule endoscopy** is creating new hybrid roles (engineering + clinical AI)
- **Graduate degrees are essentially required for R&D roles** in medical devices; BS-level candidates typically enter via support, sales, or BMET pathways
- **EU MDR (Dec 2024) + EU AI Act (Aug 2026)** double compliance burden is driving regulatory hiring, especially for companies seeking European market expansion
